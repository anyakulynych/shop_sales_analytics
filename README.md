# E-commerce Sales Analysis - SQL, Python & Tableau

## 📊 Project Overview

This project presents an end-to-end analysis of an e-commerce business using **SQL, Python, statistical analysis, and Tableau**.

The main objective was to analyze sales performance, customer behavior, traffic sources, geographical trends, and relationships between key business metrics.

The project follows a complete data analytics workflow:

**BigQuery → SQL → Data Preparation → Exploratory Data Analysis → Statistical Analysis → Visualization → Tableau Dashboard**

--

## 🎯 Business Objectives

The analysis aims to answer key business questions:

* Which continents and countries generate the highest sales?
* Which product categories contribute the most to revenue?
* How does sales performance differ across countries?
* Which devices and traffic channels generate the most sales?
* How do registered and unregistered users differ in their sales behavior?
* How do sales change over time?
* Are there relationships between traffic channels, continents, product categories, sessions, and sales?
* Are observed differences between customer groups statistically significant?
* What business insights can be derived from the analysis?

## 👤 Registered vs. Unregistered Users

Customer behavior was analyzed by separating sessions into:

* Registered users
* Unregistered users

The project examined:

* Sales generated by each group
* Distribution of daily sales
* Statistical differences between the groups

A **Mann–Whitney U test** was used because the samples did not satisfy the assumptions required for a parametric comparison.

The test produced:

* **U-statistic = 352**
* **p-value < 0.005**

Therefore, the null hypothesis was rejected, indicating a statistically significant difference between daily sales of registered and unregistered users.

# 📊 Statistical Analysis

Statistical methods were used to distinguish meaningful patterns from random variation.

## 1. Sessions vs. Sales

The relationship between the number of daily sessions and total daily sales was investigated using **Pearson correlation**.

The analysis showed a strong positive and statistically significant relationship.

This indicates that days with more sessions generally tend to have higher sales.

However, correlation does not establish a causal relationship.

---

## 2. Correlation Between Continents

Sales dynamics across the three leading continents were compared:

* Europe
* Asia
* Americas

The analysis showed very strong positive and statistically significant relationships between sales on the major continents.

This suggests that sales across regions tend to move together rather than independently, potentially reflecting common factors such as seasonality, promotions, or overall changes in customer demand.

---

## 3. Correlation Between Traffic Channels

Sales dynamics were compared across:

* Organic
* Direct
* CPC
* Referral

Pearson correlation coefficients ranged from **0.907 to 0.969**, with all relationships statistically significant.

The strongest relationship was observed between:

* **Organic and Referral: r = 0.969**
* **Organic and Direct: r = 0.965**

The weakest, while still very strong, was between:

* **CPC and Referral: r = 0.907**

This indicates that sales across the main traffic channels tend to increase and decrease together.

---

## 4. Product Category Correlations

The sales dynamics of the top 5 product categories were compared using Pearson correlation.

Weekly sales were aggregated by category and then compared pairwise to identify categories whose performance moved together over time.

---

# 🧪 Statistical Tests

Several statistical tests were applied depending on the characteristics of the data.

### Mann–Whitney U Test

Used to compare daily sales between registered and unregistered users.

**Result:** statistically significant difference between groups.

### Kruskal–Wallis Test

Used to compare the number of sessions across traffic channels.

**H = 293.023, p < 0.05**

The result indicates statistically significant differences in session volume between at least some traffic channels.

### Two-Proportion Z-Test

Used to compare the proportion of organic traffic sessions between Europe and the Americas.

**Z = 0.069, p = 0.945**

No statistically significant difference was found between the two regions.

# 💡 Key Insights

### 1. Sales and website traffic are strongly connected

There is a strong statistically significant positive relationship between the number of sessions and total sales.

This suggests that increasing qualified website traffic can potentially have a substantial impact on sales volume.

### 2. Traffic channels move together

Sales across Organic, Direct, CPC, and Referral channels demonstrated very strong correlations.

This suggests that overall customer demand or shared marketing factors may influence multiple acquisition channels simultaneously.

### 3. Registered and unregistered users behave differently

The Mann–Whitney U test showed a statistically significant difference between daily sales generated by registered and unregistered users.

This indicates that user status may be associated with different purchasing behavior.

### 4. Traffic channel performance differs significantly

The Kruskal–Wallis test confirmed statistically significant differences in session volumes between traffic channels.

Therefore, traffic channels should not be treated as equally effective sources of website activity.

### 5. Europe and the Americas have similar organic traffic shares

The two-proportion Z-test did not identify a statistically significant difference in the proportion of organic sessions between Europe and the Americas.

This suggests that the observed difference between the two regions can reasonably be attributed to sampling variation.

### 6. Delivery time is not strongly associated with profit

An additional analysis examined delivery time and profit.

The Pearson correlation was only **0.0603**, indicating an extremely weak positive relationship. Therefore, delivery time does not appear to be an important standalone predictor of profitability in this dataset.
